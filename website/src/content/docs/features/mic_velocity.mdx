Mic velocity makes the pads velocity sensitive. The Deluge listens to the tap each press makes through its built-in
microphone, and plays the note louder or softer to match how hard you hit the pad.

It works on:

- the pads in keyboard view, in every layout. In the velocity drums layout, drums wider than one pad keep taking their
  velocity from where you press them; drums one pad wide use mic velocity.
- the audition pads (the right-hand sidebar column) in clip view and automation view.

Presses that place notes on the grid in clip view aren't affected: new notes still take the default velocity.

The velocity is used everywhere a velocity-sensitive keyboard's would be: by the sound itself, on MIDI and CV out, in
recordings, and by the arpeggiator.

## Before you start

- Nothing can be plugged into the `MIC` or `LINE IN` sockets. Either one switches the built-in microphone off, and
  presses play at the default velocity until it's unplugged again.
- It works whether you're listening on headphones, through the line outputs, or on the built-in speaker. The Deluge
  takes most of its own sound back out of what the microphone hears, but with the speaker loud, very soft taps can
  be drowned out. They then play at `MIN VELOCITY`.
- Calibrate with the Deluge where you usually play it. If velocities ever feel off, for example after moving it
  somewhere new, calibrate again.

## Calibrating

`SETTINGS > PADS > MIC VELOCITY > CALIBRATE` takes 20 taps.

<Steps>

1. The centre pad lights up blue. Tap it twice, as softly as you'll ever play.

2. It turns red. Tap it twice, as hard as you'll ever play.

3. Do the same on each corner pad as it lights up. Finished pads turn green, and the left sidebar column fills up as
   you go.

   If the two taps of one strength came out too different, or your soft and hard taps too alike, the display says
   so and the same pad lights up again.

4. Now tap any pad to try it out. It lights up as brightly as it played, and the display shows its velocity.

5. Press `SELECT` to save the calibration. This also turns mic velocity on.

</Steps>

`BACK` leaves at any point without saving, keeping the calibration you had before.

## Settings

All in `SETTINGS > PADS > MIC VELOCITY`. They're saved when you leave the settings menu.

| Setting        | What it does                                                                                             | Range     | Default |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------- | ------- |
| `CALIBRATE`    | Teaches the Deluge how your taps sound (see above).                                                      |           |         |
| `ENABLED`      | Turns mic velocity on or off. Only shown once you've calibrated.                                         | ON / OFF  |         |
| `MIN VELOCITY` | The velocity your softest calibrated taps, and anything softer, play at.                                 | 1 to 127  | 20      |
| `MAX VELOCITY` | The velocity your hardest calibrated taps, and anything harder, play at.                                 | 1 to 127  | 127     |
| `CURVE`        | How quickly velocity rises from soft to hard. Higher makes lighter taps louder; lower needs harder taps. | -50 to 50 | 25      |

`CURVE` doesn't move the two ends: soft taps always play at `MIN VELOCITY` and hard ones at `MAX VELOCITY`. It changes
what happens in between. For a tap halfway between your soft and hard ones, with `MIN VELOCITY` 1 and
`MAX VELOCITY` 127:

| `CURVE`  | -50 | -25 | 0   | 25  | 50  |
| -------- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Velocity | 9   | 32  | 64  | 90  | 107 |

`MIN VELOCITY`, `MAX VELOCITY` and `CURVE` can be changed at any time without calibrating again.

## Good to know

- A press can be held back by up to a few milliseconds while the Deluge waits for the sound of the tap.
- Pads pressed together usually play at the same velocity.
- A press the microphone can't pick out (a very gentle tap, or one in a noisy room or under a loud song on the
  speaker) plays at `MIN VELOCITY`.
- The calibration and settings are kept on the SD card, in `SETTINGS/MicVelocity.XML`.
