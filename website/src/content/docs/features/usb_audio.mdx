<Badge text="c1.4 Feature" variant="tip" />

The Deluge can act as a USB audio interface. When it's connected to a computer over its USB type-B port, it shows up
as a class-compliant 4-in, 4-out audio device alongside its existing USB MIDI ports, so no driver is needed. You can:

- play audio from your computer through the Deluge's outputs
- record the Deluge's line/mic input and its main mix into your computer, at the same time
- record and monitor a second stereo stream from your computer in an audio clip, or sample it into a synth or kit

## Channels

| Channels | From computer to Deluge                              | From Deluge to computer |
| -------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| 1/2      | Mixed straight into the Deluge's outputs             | Line/mic input          |
| 3/4      | An input for audio clips and the sampler (`USB 3/4`) | Main mix                |

Your computer may list the channels as front left/right (1/2) and surround left/right (3/4). They're really two stereo
pairs.

## Format

|             | Both directions |
| ----------- | --------------- |
| Channels    | 4               |
| Sample rate | 44.1 kHz        |
| Bit depth   | 24-bit          |

The Deluge's own sample clock sets the rate for audio going to the computer. For audio coming from the computer, the
Deluge offers two modes, and the computer picks whichever it supports:

- **Following the Deluge's clock.** Computers that support this (such as macOS) send audio at exactly the Deluge's
  rate, so it's passed straight through.
- **Following the computer's clock.** Computers that can't follow the Deluge's clock, such as Windows, send at their
  own rate instead. The Deluge occasionally drops or repeats a single sample to stay in step, which isn't normally
  audible.

<Aside type="caution">Windows support hasn't been confirmed yet.</Aside>

## Setting up

1. Connect the Deluge to your computer with a USB cable.
2. Choose the Deluge as an audio input and/or output device in your computer's sound settings or your DAW. On macOS
   it's listed in Audio MIDI Setup.

The Deluge's USB MIDI ports keep working as before.

## Turning USB audio off

USB audio is on by default. To turn it off, go to `SETTINGS > USB AUDIO` (`USBA` on 7SEG). The Deluge then connects
to your computer as a USB MIDI device only, exactly as it did before USB audio was added.

The setting takes effect the next time the Deluge is turned on, since your computer only finds out what kind of
device the Deluge is when it first connects.

<Aside>
  If you've used the Deluge over USB MIDI before, your computer may treat it as
  a new device the first time you connect it with this firmware, since it now
  describes itself differently. Unplugging and reconnecting it should sort out
  any problems with it not appearing.
</Aside>

## Playing audio from your computer

Channels 1/2 from the computer are mixed straight into the Deluge's main outputs and headphones, after the master volume
and effects.

They're **not** sent back to the computer as part of the Deluge's main mix, so you won't get an echo.

## Recording the Deluge into your computer

The computer receives two stereo pairs from the Deluge:

- **Channels 1/2: the line/mic input**, exactly as it arrives - whatever's plugged into the input, or the internal mic
  if nothing is. It's sent whether or not the Deluge is monitoring it.
- **Channels 3/4: the main mix**, the same audio that goes to the main outputs, including any monitored inputs, but not
  channels 1/2 from the computer.

Both are lined up with each other the same way the Deluge's own input monitoring is, so a part recorded from the line
input on 1/2 sits against the mix on 3/4 just as you heard it.

## Recording audio from your computer in an audio clip

Channels 3/4 from the computer can be chosen as the source for an audio clip, like the line input or another track:

1. In an audio clip, open the Audio Clip Sound Menu (:key[Shift + Select]).
2. Go to `AUDIO SOURCE` and select `USB 3/4` (`USB` on 7SEG).

`USB 3/4` is always a stereo source. Unlike the line input, it isn't normalized after recording, since the computer's
audio already arrives at the level it was meant to be.

Monitoring follows the audio output mode, as for any other source (see
[Select Audio Clip Source and Monitoring](/features/community_features#317---select-audio-clip-source-and-monitoring)):
`SAMPLER` and `LOOPER` monitor `USB 3/4`, while `PLAYER` doesn't.

<Aside type="caution">
  If an audio clip is monitoring `USB 3/4`, the computer's audio becomes part of
  the Deluge's main mix, which is sent back to the computer on channels 3/4. If
  your DAW is also playing that back out to the Deluge on channels 3/4, that
  makes a feedback loop. Turn off input monitoring in the DAW, or use the
  `PLAYER` mode on the clip.
</Aside>

## Sampling audio from your computer into a synth or kit

Recording a sample from a synth or kit's sample menu uses the line input by default. To record channels 3/4 from the
computer instead, turn on `SETTINGS > RECORDING > SAMPLE FROM USB 3/4` (`SUSB` on 7SEG). Like an audio clip, it's
recorded in stereo and not normalized.

## Latency

Audio from the computer is buffered by about 6 ms (256 samples) on the Deluge before it's played or recorded, which
keeps it smooth when the Deluge is busy. Audio going to the computer is buffered by about 3 ms, on top of the line
input's usual monitoring delay for channels 1/2 - part of that is a packet kept ready in advance, so the stream carries
on smoothly even when a heavy song keeps the Deluge too busy to send one on time. Your computer adds its
own buffering on top of both, which you can usually set in your DAW.
