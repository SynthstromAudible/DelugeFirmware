- [] a resumed FREEZE_WITH_ERROR can leave the OLED and the CV outputs stuck until reboot. `deluge_display_freeze` (src/bsp/rust/src/peripherals/display.rs) polls the PIC ring for the select-knob press and discards every other byte, including the PIC's OLED chip-select echoes (248 SELECT / 249 DESELECT) that deluge-sdk's `deluge_bsp::pic` latches for `wait_oled_selected` / `wait_oled_deselected`. If the freeze lands while `display::oled_render` is suspended in one of those waits (it holds the RSPI0 bus guard across the whole select→DMA→deselect sequence), its echo is swallowed: after resume `oled_render` waits forever holding RSPI0, the panel keeps showing the error screen (so the "Operation resumed. Save to new file" popup never appears and the save UI can't be navigated), and `cv_gate::cv_writer` blocks on the bus, so CV stops updating. Pre-existing — the pre-ownership `rza1l_hal::uart::try_read_dma` loop behaved the same; found in the deluge-sdk Phase 4c-1 final review (2026-10-15). Not fixed then because the cheap fix — re-latching the select/deselect flags once on resume — can desync the handshake (a stale latch lets the next `wait_oled_selected` return before the PIC has asserted CS, so frames go out unselected, and the offset can persist), and blindly forwarding echoes during the freeze would also latch the echoes of `oled::draw_blocking`'s own select/deselect. A fix needs a designed re-sync, e.g. tracking the expected echo in the freeze loop and forwarding only the one `oled_render` is actually waiting for, or giving `oled_render` a timeout-and-reselect path, plus a hardware check: trigger a freeze while the OLED is actively redrawing, resume with the select knob, and confirm the popup appears and CV still moves. **Possibly fixed by deluge-sdk 730e6383 (pinned in baf042186):** `oled_select_and_wait` / `oled_deselect_and_wait` resend the command every 50 ms until it is echoed, clearing any earlier echo before each send, so a swallowed echo now costs one resend rather than the bus. Unverified: needs the hardware check above.
- [] DSP perf left from porting upstream's #5160/#5143/#5148 (bit-exact speedups of the legacy code dsp-next replaced; revisit after the voice-kernel lift, as each needs a dsp-next framework change). Measured on the rig with the DSP bench (`DELUGE_DSP_BENCH_CASES=filters|delay`; `.superpowers/estate/dsp-perf-ports/run_bench.sh` + `compare.py`; cycle counts are deterministic). Taken so far: LP ladder UNRESONANT mode (-23 to -37%), HP ladder morph-0 mode (-13 to -27%), analog delay `AnalogImpulseResponse::step_pair` (-6 to -15%). Open:
  - **The LP ladder's noise seed in registers.** The ladder draws from its block-local Context, so the per-sample load and store of a global seed are gone. Rig-benched against `5d303d79b` (2026-10-30): most mono, resonant and 24 dB cases are 2-8% faster, but the unresonant stereo loops are slower (12 dB +14%, 24 dB +8.5%) and the drive mono ones +3-10%: the seed, shared by both lanes, now competes for registers in the loops and spills to the stack every sample, and the step constants are rebuilt each iteration. Over all 24 low-pass and filter-set cases the change is a small net gain (geometric mean -0.55%, total -0.7%; 14 faster, 9 slower): the swings between modes are the shared-function register allocation recorded above, not a cost of the per-block stream. Tried and worse: drawing the block's noise ahead into a buffer and reading it through a cursor (up to +24.5%). If the unresonant stereo loops matter, look at that function's register allocation per mode; bench with `DELUGE_DSP_BENCH_CASES=filters`.
  - **The fed-back LP ladders are up to 4.7% slower than before the UNRESONANT modes** (24dB stereo, 24dB resonant mono): the loops share one function's register allocation and spill a little more. Tried without effect: outlining each mode's loop (by value and as local copies, both spilled more), `-falign-loops=32`, the legacy code shape. Also measured: choosing the input's saturation per block instead of per sample made the fed-back loops 5-13% slower on the A9 (fewer instructions, but the per-sample test was filling multiply latency).
  - **A NEON analog impulse response:** left and right as one 2-lane vector per tap (upstream uses vqrdmulh by half-taps, exact since every tap is even). Upstream's block convolution form is ruled out: the delay's fused per-frame order is what fixed the short-delay lap. The NEON-to-ARM transfer per frame may eat the gain; bench before keeping it.
