The idiom lowers to plain 64-bit multiply-and-shift IR, so clang's loop vectorizer widens loops of it into NEON (`vmull.s32` + `vaddhn.i64`, or per-lane `umull` with `vmov.32` shuttling) that runs 3-7× slower on the A9 (DSP bench: dry_wet_blend 9 → 61 cycles/sample, stereo_gain 4 → 24); `llvm.smul.fix*` is in the vectorizer's trivially-vectorizable list and goes the same way. An intrinsic that is opaque to the vectorizer but an ordinary instruction to the scheduler (as ACLE's `__qadd` is) is what `q31_ops.h`'s inline asm stands in for today; empty-asm register barriers on the product or the multiplicand were benched and cost ±10-40% elsewhere. Routes: (a) `int_arm_smmul`… in IntrinsicsARM.td + selection patterns + `__builtin_arm_smmul`… in BuiltinsARM.td (small, but the firmware builds with the system clang, so it needs a pinned toolchain or upstream acceptance, uncertain without ACLE); (b) fix the ARM cost model so the vectorizer stops widening i64 `mulhs` on ARMv7 NEON (the underlying bug; reproducer: `o[k] = (int32_t)(((int64_t)i[k] * g) >> 32) << 1` at -O2 -mcpu=cortex-a9); (c) a pass plugin marking such loops no-vectorize (fragile: it must load into clang and the device's LTO link, rebuilt per clang release). Bench evidence and scripts: .superpowers/estate/fixed-point-s0/bench/. A prototype of (a) and (b) exists on a local LLVM branch, arm-dsp-intrinsics (ee9ac63ad952 adds the intrinsics and builtins, e7698f20a4e4 costs v2i64 mul by its lowering). Measured on the rig with it (.superpowers/estate/fixed-point-s0/clang24/, one image, a same-code control at 1.00×): the 3-7× scalarised loops are gone, but the 2-lane `vmull.s32` + `vshrn.i64` loop it still vectorizes is 1.45-1.68× slower than scalar `smmul` (with an add: 1.34-1.42× vs `smmla`; a run-time gain: 1.24-1.37×), and the 4-lane rounded form only breaks even (0.97-1.04× vs `smmulr`), despite ~40% fewer instructions: each iteration is one dependent NEON chain at the default interleave of 1. Interleave 2 (same harness, one image): forcing width 2 × interleave 2 (two `vmull` chains per iteration) still loses or ties (mulh 1.20-1.25×, mla 0.97-1.07×, mulr 1.04-1.23×; only the constant-gain loop wins, 0.81-0.90×), but with interleave 2 left to the cost model it picks width 1 — a scalar loop unrolled ×2 (`ldrd`, two independent `smmul`, `strd`) — and that is 22-30% FASTER than the plain scalar loop (mulh 0.74×, mulr 0.78×, gainrt 0.70× at n=128). So the A9 lever is scalar interleaving (VF=1, IC=2) for these loops, which clang's default (interleave 1) does not do, not vectorization; the cost model must also stop picking the mixed vector-load + scalar-`smmul` + `vmov.32` loop it chose for the accumulating form (1.50-1.55× slower).
